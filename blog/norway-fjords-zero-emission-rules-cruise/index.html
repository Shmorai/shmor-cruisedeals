Norwegian fjords cruises and the new zero-emission rules: what travellers should know

Norway has begun phasing in zero-emission requirements for ships in two of its best-known UNESCO-listed fjords. If you are planning a fjords cruise for 2027 or later, here is what the rules say, which ships they affect now, and what to ask before you book.

What are Norway's zero-emission fjord rules?

According to Business Norway (article updated 22 October 2026), the rules cover the UNESCO World Heritage fjords of Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord. Since 1 January 2026, passenger ships below 10,000 gross tonnage must operate using energy sources that produce no direct carbon dioxide or methane emissions. From 1 January 2032 the same requirement applies to ships of 10,000 gross tonnage and above. Cruise operators must also connect vessels to shore power where it is available.

The article says the rules apply to ships operating in the fjords and to those that pass through them, and that the regulation is technology neutral, so it does not prescribe one energy solution.

Which cruise ships does it affect now?

The 2026 requirement is for ships under 10,000 gross tonnage, which means smaller vessels such as expedition-style and small-ship operators and coastal passenger ships. Bigger cruise ships fall under the 2032 date. That does not mean larger ships have no obligations today: shore power connection applies where available. Because ship sizes and operators' plans change, ask your cruise line how its ship will meet the rules on the dates you plan to sail rather than assuming.

Will my Norwegian fjords itinerary change?

The official sources we reviewed do not say that fares or itineraries must change, and we won't guess. What they do show is that operators are investing in new approaches: Business Norway describes hybrid ships that can sail for up to four hours without emissions, and small electric boats in the fjords. Itineraries can also change for weather or port reasons at any time, so check the specific sailing's terms. Our Norwegian fjords cruises page explains the best season and ports.

What to ask before you book a fjords cruise

  • Which fjords does the itinerary actually sail into, and which does it only visit by tender or from a nearby port?
  • How will the ship meet the zero-emission or shore power requirements on my sailing dates?
  • What happens to the itinerary if a ship cannot comply or the weather stops a visit?
  • Is the departure from a UK port? See our cruises from Southampton guide.

Frequently asked questions

Are cruise ships banned from the Norwegian fjords?
No. Business Norway says the rules require zero direct CO2 and methane emissions in Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord: from 1 January 2026 for ships under 10,000 gross tonnage and from 1 January 2032 for larger ships.
Do big cruise ships have to meet the rule in 2026?
Not yet. The 10,000 gross tonnage and above requirement starts on 1 January 2032, but shore power must be used where available.
Which fjords do the rules cover?
Business Norway names the UNESCO World Heritage fjords Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord.
