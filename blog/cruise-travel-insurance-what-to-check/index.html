Cruise travel insurance: what to check before you sail

A cruise can take you a long way from the nearest hospital, and some of the costs of things going wrong are easy to underestimate. Here is what official guidance says to look for in a policy, and the questions to ask before you pay.

Do you need travel insurance for a cruise?

The UK government’s advice is that if you travel internationally you should buy appropriate travel insurance before you go, because without it you could be liable for emergency expenses, including medical treatment, that may run to thousands of pounds. GOV.UK gives illustrative examples of costs without cover, such as £25,000+ for a broken leg needing hospital treatment in Spain, and £150,000+ for an infection treated in a hospital in the USA with possible evacuation (figures as of November 2026).

For cruises, GOV.UK adds a specific point: check the booking conditions of the operator you plan to sail with, as cruises generally require an additional level of cover because it is more difficult to get to hospital for treatment. Read them before you buy a policy. If you’re still choosing a sailing, start with our first-time cruise guide.

Buy it soon after booking

GOV.UK advises buying travel insurance as soon as possible after booking your trip. Cruise fares can be paid in stages, so check the cancellation terms that apply to your deposit and final payment.

What your cruise policy should cover

Based on the official checklist, look for cover for:

  • The full length of your trip. Many policies have a maximum trip length or an annual limit on days abroad.
  • Medical treatment in state or private hospitals, including emergency transport such as an ambulance, which is often charged separately.
  • Emergency travel home on medical grounds and getting home after treatment if you can’t use your original ticket.
  • Repatriation costs if you or a family member dies abroad.
  • All the places you’ll visit, even in transit. On a cruise that includes each port of call and any flights.
  • The activities you’ll do, such as excursions or adventure sports. Some need an add-on.
  • A 24-hour assistance helpline.

Pre-existing conditions: declare everything

GOV.UK says to declare existing conditions, and any pending treatment or tests, so you’re covered if related complications happen on the trip. Failing to declare something may invalidate your travel insurance. Mental health conditions must also be declared, and some policies exclude treatment for pre-existing mental health conditions. If you have a serious medical condition, you may need a specialist provider; the British Insurance Brokers’ Association (BIBA) has a travel medical directory.

Older travellers should also check age limits, especially on annual policies.

Exclusions that catch people out

The official guidance lists things to check in the small print:

  • Alcohol and drugs: most policies don’t cover events after excessive alcohol or recreational drugs.
  • Destinations where the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) advises against all or all but essential travel, which may invalidate cover.
  • Sports such as jet skiing or skydiving, and hiring quad bikes or mopeds, which usually need extra cover. This matters on shore excursions.
  • Natural disasters, civil unrest and strikes, which may be limited or excluded.
  • Driving: if you hire a car at a port, check what you’re covered for with the hire company.

Hurricane season is a particular consideration in the Caribbean; see our hurricane season cruise tips.

GHIC and EHIC aren’t a substitute

A UK Global Health Insurance Card (GHIC) or European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) gives access to state-provided, medically necessary healthcare in the EU, Switzerland and some other countries on the same terms as residents. GOV.UK is clear they are not alternatives to travel insurance: they don’t cover private healthcare costs, repatriation or extra costs such as mountain rescue. Some insurers may waive the excess on medical treatment if you use one, so check your policy.

Cruise line or cruise operator failure

GOV.UK notes that cover if an airline or travel agent goes out of business is typically not included in standard policies. If your cruise includes flights, ATOL protection (UK) or a policy that includes supplier failure cover can matter; see our cruise deals guide for ways to compare what’s included. US travellers should check what their own policy and payment method cover and read the operator’s booking terms.

Frequently asked questions

Is travel insurance compulsory for a cruise?
Requirements vary by cruise line. GOV.UK advises checking the booking conditions of your operator because cruises generally need an additional level of cover. Check the operator’s terms before you book.
Does a GHIC cover me on a cruise?
A GHIC or EHIC gives access to state-provided healthcare in participating countries, but it is not travel insurance and doesn’t cover private treatment, repatriation or some other costs. Take out a travel insurance policy as well.
What happens if I don’t declare a medical condition?
GOV.UK warns that failing to declare something may invalidate your travel insurance. Declare existing conditions and any pending tests or treatment.
