What to pack for a cruise: a practical checklist

Pack once, pack right. This checklist covers documents, clothes for different climates and dress codes, embarkation-day essentials and the items cruise lines commonly ban.

By the CruiseDeals editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 1 min read

Documents and money

  • Passport, plus any visas or travel authorisations your route needs (see our entry rules guide).
  • Cruise booking confirmation, boarding documents and luggage tags.
  • Travel insurance details and an emergency contact list.
  • A credit or debit card for your onboard account, and a little local currency for ports.

Your embarkation-day bag

Your main luggage may not reach your cabin until later in the day, so carry a small bag with:

  • Documents and any medication.
  • Swimwear, sunscreen and sunglasses.
  • Phone, charger and a change of clothes.

Clothes for the ship and the ports

  • Check your cruise line’s dress code. Some have formal or “dressy” evenings, while others are casual every night.
  • Comfortable walking shoes for ports, plus sandals for deck.
  • A light jacket or layers, because air conditioning and sea breezes can be cool even in the tropics.
  • For Alaska, the fjords or polar voyages: warm layers, a waterproof jacket, hat and gloves.
  • Modest clothing for visiting religious sites in some ports.

Useful extras

  • Seasickness remedies, even if you don’t expect to need them.
  • A reusable water bottle and a small backpack for port days.
  • Binoculars for scenic and wildlife cruising.
  • A non-surge-protected multi-socket or USB charger if your line allows one, since cabins can have few sockets.

Items cruise lines commonly ban

Each line publishes its own list, so always check it. Commonly prohibited items include clothes irons and steamers, candles, surge-protected extension leads, weapons, drones (or limits on their use) and, on many lines, your own alcohol beyond any stated allowance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bring my own alcohol on a cruise?
It depends on the line. Some allow a limited amount of wine at embarkation, some charge a corkage fee and others don’t allow any. Check your line’s policy.
Do I need formal wear on a cruise?
Only if your line has formal nights and you want to join in. Many lines now have relaxed dress codes. Check before you pack.
