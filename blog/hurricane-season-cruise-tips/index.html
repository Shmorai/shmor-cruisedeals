Cruising in hurricane season: what to know before you book

Caribbean and Bahamas cruises sail all year, including hurricane season. Here’s when the season runs, what happens when a storm forms, and how US, UK and EU cruisers can protect their trip.

By the CruiseDeals editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 3 min read

When is hurricane season in the Caribbean?

According to the US National Hurricane Center (part of NOAA), the Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November. Its statistical peak is around 10 September, with most activity between mid-August and mid-October.

The season affects the Caribbean, the Bahamas, Bermuda, the Gulf of Mexico and the US East Coast, so it matters for most Caribbean cruises from Florida and other US ports, and for fly-cruises from the UK and Europe. Storms are never certain on any given sailing, and many cruises in the season sail normally.

What happens to a cruise when a hurricane forms?

Unlike a beach resort, a ship can move. Cruise lines track storms closely and, when one threatens, usually change the itinerary instead of cancelling. That can mean:

  • visiting different ports, or the same ports in a different order
  • extra days at sea
  • leaving or returning a day early or late, if a home port is closed
  • rough seas for part of the trip, even when the ship avoids the storm itself

Most cruise contracts allow the line to change ports and itineraries for safety and weather, often without compensation. Read your cruise line’s terms before you book so you know what to expect.

Insurance and your refund rights

  • Buy travel insurance when you book. Many policies won’t cover a storm once it has been named, because it’s then a known event. Check whether yours covers missed departures, delays and itinerary changes caused by weather.
  • UK cruisers: if your cruise is a package (for example a fly-cruise, or a cruise with hotel nights), the Package Travel Regulations 2018 apply. If unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances at the destination significantly affect the package, you can cancel before departure without a termination fee and get a full refund. Flight-inclusive packages sold in the UK should also be ATOL protected.
  • EU cruisers: the EU Package Travel Directive gives similar rights for packages.
  • US cruisers: your rights mostly come from the cruise line’s contract and your insurance policy, so read both carefully.

Tips for booking a hurricane season cruise

  • Fly in a day early. Storms can disrupt flights as well as ships, and arriving the day before sailing gives you a buffer.
  • Consider timing. June, July and November usually see less activity than the peak months, though storms can form at any point in the season.
  • Think about itinerary. The Southern Caribbean islands near South America are generally considered to lie south of the main hurricane belt, though not immune.
  • Stay flexible. Have a backup plan for shore excursions and let family know your plans may change.
  • Watch official forecasts from the National Hurricane Center in the days before you sail, and follow your cruise line’s updates.

Prefer to avoid the season altogether? Winter is the main Caribbean season, and the Canary Islands offer mild winter sun closer to Europe. For short-notice sailings, see last-minute cruises.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to cruise during hurricane season?
Cruise lines monitor storms and change itineraries to keep ships away from them, so the main risk is disruption rather than danger: changed ports, extra sea days, rough seas or delayed departures.
Will I get a refund if a hurricane changes my cruise?
It depends on your cruise contract, your insurance and where you booked. Itinerary changes are usually allowed without compensation. UK and EU package travellers have extra cancellation rights in some circumstances.
What is the worst month to cruise the Caribbean?
The National Hurricane Center says most Atlantic hurricane activity happens between mid-August and mid-October, with the peak around 10 September.
