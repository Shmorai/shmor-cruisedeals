Barcelona's cruise passenger tax: what is planned and what it means for your trip

Barcelona is one of the Mediterranean's busiest cruise ports, and the city has been moving to raise the tourist charge on cruise passengers who only stop for the day. Reports differ on timing, so here is what has been reported, what is still to be confirmed and how to check before you book.

What is changing in Barcelona?

Barcelona already charges cruise passengers who spend under 12 hours in the city. Reports from 2026 describe a municipal surcharge of €5 on top of a €6 charge collected by the Catalan government, so about €11 per passenger. Catalan law has been changed to let the city raise its own part to a maximum of €24, which would bring the combined total to €30.

That is the ceiling. What matters for your booking is how quickly the city moves towards it.

When does it start? Reports differ

Reports from late September 2026 (Ara and Catalan News) described a phased plan: the city's part rising to about €12 in 2027 (€18 in total with the regional charge), then in steps to €24 by 2030 (€30 in total). The city council's economy committee backed it, and a final vote was reported as pending. Earlier press coverage in July described an immediate rise to the maximum, so sources disagree. We have not found the final ordinance, so treat all figures as reported, as of late September 2026, and check with your cruise line or the Barcelona authorities before you rely on them.

Who pays?

The charge is aimed at transit passengers: those who spend under 12 hours in Barcelona without staying overnight. One report said cruises that begin or end in Barcelona stay exempt from the higher rate; another did not mention exemptions, so confirm how your own itinerary would be treated. Deputy mayor Jordi Valls was quoted as saying the aim is "not to increase revenue, but to deter this type of offer".

Why the city is doing it, and who disagrees

Ara reported that the council projects up to €23.7 million in extra revenue by 2031 if passenger numbers hold, but the deputy mayor said the main aim is to deter short cruise calls rather than raise money. The proposal passed with support from some parties and opposition from others: Barcelona en Comú voted against because it wanted the full €24 straight away, while the PP and Vox voted against, saying it harms tourism. Because it is still a live political issue, details could change.

For travellers the point is straightforward: short stops in popular, crowded ports are the stops cities are targeting. If you value a full day ashore, itineraries with longer or overnight stays are worth comparing, though you should check whether any local charge would apply to them.

Will it change what I pay?

It depends on how your booking handles government and port charges. Some cruise fares include them; others add them as a separate line at booking or on board. Before you pay, ask whether taxes and port charges are already in the quoted price and whether the cruise line can pass on changes after you book. Read the booking terms rather than assuming.

For comparison, you can see how total prices differ across lines on our cruise deals page, and which departures include longer stays in port on Mediterranean cruises.

How to plan around it

  • Check the itinerary: a port call under 12 hours is the group affected. Starting or ending your cruise in Barcelona is a different situation.
  • Compare several routes: Greek, Italian and French ports are alternatives; see Greek Islands cruises.
  • Book at the right time: our guide to wave season explains when lines discount.
  • Keep entry rules in mind: see EES, ETIAS and the UK ETA.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Barcelona cruise passenger tax?
As reported in 2026, passengers on calls under 12 hours pay about €11 today (€5 city plus €6 regional). The city's part could rise to a maximum of €24, making €30 in total, but the timing is reported to be phased.
Do I pay if Barcelona is my departure port?
One report said cruises beginning or ending in Barcelona remain exempt from the higher rate. Another did not mention exemptions, so check with your cruise line.
Is the Barcelona cruise tax confirmed?
Catalan law now allows the higher ceiling, and the city council's committee backed the increase. A final city vote and the effective dates were still being reported as of late September 2026, so check official notices.
