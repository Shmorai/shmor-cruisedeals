Do you need a passport for a cruise? Closed-loop cruises, REAL ID and more

Some US cruisers can sail without a passport book, but it isn’t always a good idea. Here’s who can, which documents count, and what UK and EU cruisers need, as of October 2026.

By the CruiseDeals editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 3 min read

  2. Can US citizens cruise without a passport?

What is a closed-loop cruise?

US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) defines a closed-loop cruise as one that begins and ends at the same US port, for example a round trip from Miami to the Bahamas and back to Miami. A sailing that starts in one US port and ends in another, or starts or ends abroad, is not closed-loop.

Many short Caribbean cruises and Bahamas cruises from Florida, Texas and other US ports are closed-loop. So are many round-trip Alaska cruises from Seattle, which usually include a Canadian port call.

Can US citizens cruise without a passport?

Often, yes. As of October 2026, CBP says US citizens on closed-loop cruises can re-enter the United States with a birth certificate and a government-issued photo ID. Other documents CBP accepts for US citizens arriving by sea include:

  • a US passport book
  • a US passport card
  • an Enhanced Driver’s License (issued by only a few states)
  • a Trusted Traveler card (NEXUS, SENTRI or FAST)

CBP says US citizen children under 16 need only a birth certificate or other proof of citizenship when entering by sea.

But the closed-loop rule only covers getting back into the US. CBP warns that the countries your ship visits may still require a passport, and cruise lines can set stricter rules than the legal minimum. Always check your cruise line’s document requirements for your exact itinerary.

Why many cruisers take a passport anyway

A US passport book is the only one of these documents CBP lists for flying back into the US from abroad (apart from a few specialist cards). The passport card, Enhanced Driver’s License and birth certificate don’t work for international flights. That matters if things go wrong:

  • You miss the ship in a foreign port and have to fly to the next one or fly home.
  • You need to leave the cruise early because of illness or a family emergency.
  • The itinerary changes and the ship ends somewhere unexpected.

In those cases, getting home without a passport book can mean delays and a visit to a US embassy or consulate. If you cruise often, a passport book is the simplest choice.

Does REAL ID work for a cruise?

A REAL ID-compliant driver’s license is accepted for domestic flights and some federal facilities, but it is not proof of citizenship, and it isn’t on CBP’s list of entry documents. On a closed-loop cruise it can serve as your photo ID next to a birth certificate, but it can’t replace the birth certificate. It also can’t be used to fly abroad to join a ship.

UK and EU cruisers: what you need

The closed-loop exception applies only to US citizens. If you’re a UK or EU citizen, plan on taking a valid passport on every cruise, and check what each country on the itinerary needs:

  • Cruises from US ports: UK and most EU citizens travelling under the Visa Waiver Program need an approved ESTA (or a US visa), whether they arrive by air or by sea. Apply only on the official ESTA website.
  • Cruises in Europe: UK citizens are registered by the EU’s Entry/Exit System at Schengen ports. See EES, ETIAS and the UK ETA for cruise passengers.
  • Cruises from the UK: most visitors who don’t need a visa, including US and most EU citizens, need a UK ETA to join a ship in Southampton or another UK port.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a passport for a Caribbean cruise?
US citizens on a closed-loop cruise (starting and ending at the same US port) can re-enter the US with a birth certificate and photo ID, but some destinations and cruise lines still require a passport. UK and EU citizens need a passport.
Can I go on a cruise with just my REAL ID?
No. A REAL ID is not proof of citizenship. On a closed-loop cruise, US citizens can use it as photo ID together with a birth certificate, if their cruise line and destinations allow.
Is a passport card enough for a cruise?
CBP accepts the US passport card for re-entry by sea, but it can’t be used for international flights. If you have to fly home from a foreign port, you’ll need a passport book.
Do children need a passport for a closed-loop cruise?
CBP says US citizen children under 16 need only a birth certificate or other proof of citizenship to enter by sea. Check your cruise line’s rules too.
