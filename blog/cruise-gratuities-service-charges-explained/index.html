Cruise gratuities explained: how daily tips work and how to budget for them

Gratuities are one of the extras that can make a cruise fare look cheaper than the real cost. Most big lines add a daily amount per guest, plus percentages on drinks and spa services. Here is how it works, using Royal Caribbean’s published policy as a worked example (as of November 2026) and what to check with any line.

What are cruise gratuities?

Cruise gratuities (also called service charges) are the tips for crew such as dining, bar, stateroom and hotel staff. Many lines add them to your onboard account automatically each day, rather than leaving tipping to your discretion. They are normally on top of the advertised fare, so include them when you compare prices on our cruise deals pages.

A worked example: Royal Caribbean

Royal Caribbean’s gratuities page says guests who do not prepay have a daily gratuity applied automatically: US$21.00 per guest per day for suites and US$18.50 per guest per day for all other stateroom categories, except where prohibited by law. The line says gratuities are pooled and shared among crew. It also says 18% is automatically added to beverages and beverage packages, specialty dining, room service and minibar purchases, and 20% to spa and salon services.

Other lines set different amounts and rules, and rates can change, so always read the current policy for your line and sailing.

Can you change or remove cruise gratuities?

On Royal Caribbean, gratuities are described as optional: you can modify or remove them at Guest Relations onboard at any time before the morning of disembarkation, and changes cannot be made after the cruise has ended. Policies differ by line, so check yours before you assume you can. If you prefer to tip individual crew directly, say so at Guest Relations rather than simply ignoring the charge.

How to budget gratuities into your cruise price

A simple method: multiply the daily rate by the number of nights and by every guest who is charged (check whether children are charged on your line). Then add the percentage that applies to drinks and spa services you plan to buy. Compare that total, not just the fare, when you decide between sailings. Combined with items such as travel insurance and your cabin choice, it gives you a realistic cost.

Questions to ask before you book

  • What is the daily gratuity per guest, and does it differ by cabin type?
  • Are children charged, and from what age?
  • Is there a separate service charge on drinks packages?
  • Can it be prepaid, and can it be changed onboard?
  • Which currency is it charged in?

Ask your cruise line or agent for the written policy and keep a copy with your booking confirmation.

Last updated: 27 November 2026

Frequently asked questions

Are cruise gratuities compulsory?
It depends on the line. Royal Caribbean describes its gratuities as optional and adjustable at Guest Relations before the morning of disembarkation. Check your own line’s current policy.
Are cruise gratuities included in the fare?
Usually not. Lines typically add them per guest per day on top of the fare, unless a package says otherwise, so check what your booking includes.
Is there a service charge on drinks and spa treatments?
On Royal Caribbean, 18% is added to beverages, specialty dining, room service and minibar purchases and 20% to spa and salon services, as of November 2026. Other lines differ.
