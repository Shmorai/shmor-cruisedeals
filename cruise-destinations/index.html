Cruise regions at a glance

  • Caribbean: all year; most popular from December to April. Hurricane season runs 1 June to 30 November.
  • Mediterranean: mainly spring to autumn; shoulder months are quieter.
  • Alaska: roughly May to September.
  • Norwegian fjords: roughly May to September; northern lights voyages in autumn and winter.
  • European rivers: spring to autumn, plus Christmas markets in late November and December.
  • Canary Islands and Madeira: popular in autumn and winter for mild weather. Many sail from Southampton.
  • Antarctica: roughly November to March. The Arctic: roughly June to September.
  • Asia and Australia: seasons vary by region; Australia’s main cruise season is its summer, roughly October to April.

Repositioning cruises

Twice a year, many ships move between regions, for example from the Mediterranean to the Caribbean in autumn and back in spring. These one-way repositioning cruises often have lots of sea days and can be good value per night, but check the cost of the one-way flight home.

How to choose a destination

  • Decide what matters most: beaches, culture, scenery, wildlife or the ship itself.
  • Count the sea days: some people love them, others prefer a port every day.
  • Factor in travel to the port, especially for long-haul fly-cruises.
  • Check entry requirements for every country on the route.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best place to cruise in winter?
For warmth, the Caribbean, Canary Islands, Mexico and Australia are popular in the northern winter. For something different, try a northern lights voyage along Norway’s coast or an Antarctic expedition.
Where should I go on my first cruise?
The Caribbean and the Mediterranean are classic first cruises, with plenty of ships and itineraries. A short cruise is a good way to try cruising before committing to a longer trip.
