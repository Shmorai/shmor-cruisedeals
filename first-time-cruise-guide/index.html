What a cruise fare usually includes

  • Your cabin, and travel between ports.
  • Meals in the main dining room and buffet, plus some snacks.
  • Basic drinks such as water, tea, coffee and juice at breakfast.
  • Most entertainment, pools, gyms and kids’ clubs.

Often extra: alcoholic and soft drinks (or a drinks package), specialty restaurants, Wi‑Fi, shore excursions, spa treatments, and gratuities or a daily service charge. Port taxes and fees may be shown separately from the headline fare.

Choosing a cabin

  • Inside: no window and the cheapest option. Fine if you’ll mostly be out and about.
  • Oceanview: a window or porthole that doesn’t open.
  • Balcony: your own outdoor space. Great for scenic routes like Alaska and the fjords.
  • Suite: more space, and often perks such as priority boarding or a dedicated restaurant.

Mid-ship cabins on lower decks tend to feel the least motion, which helps if you’re worried about seasickness.

Gratuities and service charges

Many cruise lines add a daily service charge or gratuity per guest to your onboard account. Others include gratuities in the fare, and the approach often differs between US and UK-focused lines. Check your line’s policy so you can budget for it.

Embarkation day and onboard tips

  • Complete online check-in before you travel and follow your arrival time slot.
  • Keep medication, documents and swimwear in a day bag, as your main luggage may arrive at your cabin later.
  • Attend the mandatory safety briefing (muster drill), which may be done partly on an app or TV.
  • Always be back onboard by the stated time in port: ships won’t wait for late passengers on independent tours.

Frequently asked questions

Will I get seasick on a cruise?
Modern ships have stabilisers and most people feel little motion, especially on calm routes such as the Caribbean or rivers. If you’re worried, choose a mid-ship cabin on a lower deck and pack a remedy.
How much spending money do I need for a cruise?
It depends on your drinks, excursions, Wi‑Fi and gratuities. Look up your line’s prices for these before you sail and set a daily budget.
Is a drinks package worth it?
It depends on how much you drink. Add up what you’d buy on a typical day at the line’s prices and compare it with the daily package cost, remembering that packages usually apply to everyone in the cabin.
